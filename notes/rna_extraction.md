# RNA Extraction (TRIzol Method)

> **Note**
>
> These notes summarize the overall workflow and concepts behind RNA extraction using the TRIzol method. They are intended as a learning resource and do **not** replace the official laboratory protocol.

---

# Objective

Extract high-quality total RNA from dissected tissue for downstream RNA sequencing and other molecular biology applications.

# Sample Preparation

### Goal

Prepare tissue while preserving RNA integrity.

### Key Points

- Keep samples cold whenever possible.
- Work quickly to minimize RNA degradation.
- RNAlater is used to stabilize RNA before extraction.

---

# Homogenization

### Goal

Completely disrupt the tissue so that all cells are exposed to the extraction reagent.

### Why It Matters

Incomplete homogenization can reduce RNA yield and extraction efficiency.

---

# Cell Lysis

### Goal

Break open cells and immediately inactivate RNases.

### Why TRIzol?

TRIzol lyses cells while protecting RNA from degradation during extraction.

---

# Phase Separation

### Goal

Separate RNA from DNA, proteins, and cellular debris.

### What Happens?

After chloroform is added and the sample is centrifuged, three layers form:

- **Upper aqueous phase** → RNA
- **Interphase** → DNA
- **Lower organic phase** → proteins and lipids

### Important

Avoid disturbing the interphase when transferring the aqueous layer.

---

# RNA Precipitation

### Goal

Recover RNA from the aqueous phase.

### Why Isopropanol?

Isopropanol causes RNA to precipitate into a pellet.

### Why Glycogen?

For very small RNA amounts, glycogen can act as a carrier and improve RNA recovery.

---

# RNA Wash

### Goal

Remove salts and remaining contaminants.

### Important

Residual ethanol can interfere with downstream applications, so it should be removed completely before resuspending the RNA.

---

# RNA Resuspension

### Goal

Dissolve purified RNA.

RNA is typically resuspended in nuclease-free water.

If the pellet is difficult to dissolve, gentle warming may help before returning the sample to ice.

---

# RNA Quality Assessment

Typical quality checks include:

- RNA concentration
- RNA purity
- RNA Integrity Number (RIN)

High-quality RNA is essential for successful RNA sequencing.

---

# Common Pitfalls

- RNase contamination
- Incomplete homogenization
- Disturbing the interphase
- Losing a small RNA pellet
- Residual ethanol after washing
- Incomplete RNA dissolution

---

# Practical Tips

- Keep everything RNase-free.
- Label tubes before starting.
- Prepare reagents in advance.
- Keep samples on ice whenever possible.
- Work carefully rather than quickly.
